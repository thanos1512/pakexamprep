Mountain ranges of Pakistan

Overview

Pakistan's mountains fall into two big groups: the giant northern ranges that meet in Gilgit-Baltistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and the lower western ranges that run along the Afghan and Iranian borders down to the sea. The Salt Range stands on its own in the middle of Punjab.

Most exam questions come from three things: which peak belongs to which range, which pass connects which places, and the "highest, longest, largest" facts.

The northern giants

Three of the world's great mountain systems meet in northern Pakistan. That is why the country has five of the world's fourteen peaks above 8,000 metres.

Karakoram

The Karakoram is the highest and most glaciated of the three. It runs along Pakistan's border with China in Gilgit-Baltistan.

  • K2 (8,611 m): Pakistan's highest peak and the second-highest in the world. Also called Mount Godwin-Austen or Chhogori. An Italian expedition first climbed it in 1954.
  • Other 8,000-metre peaks: Gasherbrum I (8,080 m), Broad Peak (8,051 m) and Gasherbrum II (8,035 m).
  • Other well-known peaks: Masherbrum (7,821 m) and Rakaposhi (7,788 m), which towers over the Hunza-Nagar valley.
  • Glaciers: Siachen (about 76 km), Biafo (about 67 km), Baltoro (about 63 km) and Batura. Few places outside the polar regions hold this much glacial ice.
  • Karakoram Highway: crosses into China at the Khunjerab Pass (4,693 m), one of the highest paved border crossings in the world.

Himalaya

The western end of the Himalaya lies in Pakistan, south of the Karakoram. It is usually described in three parallel belts:

  • Great Himalaya: the highest belt, ending in the west at Nanga Parbat (8,126 m), the world's ninth-highest peak. Its nickname is the "Killer Mountain". Hermann Buhl first climbed it in 1953.
  • Lesser Himalaya: includes the Pir Panjal range in AJK and the hill stations of Murree and the Galiyat.
  • Outer Himalaya (Siwaliks): the low foothills. The Margalla Hills behind Islamabad belong to this foothill zone.

Hindu Kush

The Hindu Kush lies to the west, along the Afghan border in Chitral.

  • Tirich Mir (7,708 m): the highest peak of the Hindu Kush, in Chitral.
  • Noshaq (7,492 m): the range's second-highest peak, on the Pakistan-Afghanistan border.
  • Passes: Lowari links Dir with Chitral and now has a road tunnel. Shandur links Chitral with Gilgit and hosts the famous Shandur polo festival.

Exam tip: examiners like to swap pairs that sound alike.

K2 is the "Savage Mountain", Nanga Parbat the "Killer Mountain". K2 was first climbed in 1954, Nanga Parbat in 1953. Bolan links Sibi with Quetta; Khojak links Quetta with Chaman.

The western ranges and the Salt Range

South of the Hindu Kush, a chain of lower, drier ranges runs along the western border all the way to the Arabian Sea. For centuries their passes were the main routes into the subcontinent from Afghanistan and Central Asia.

  • Safed Koh (Spin Ghar): along the Afghan border near Peshawar. The Khyber Pass cuts through its eastern hills, linking Peshawar with Torkham and on to Kabul.
  • Waziristan Hills: between the Kurram and Gomal rivers, crossed by the Tochi and Gomal passes.
  • Sulaiman Range: runs north to south where KP, Punjab and Balochistan meet. Its best-known peak is Takht-e-Sulaiman (about 3,487 m), and Fort Munro near Dera Ghazi Khan is its hill station.
  • Toba Kakar Range: in northern Balochistan along the Afghan border. The Khojak Pass and the British-era Khojak railway tunnel link Quetta with Chaman.
  • Central Brahui Range: around Quetta, with the Bolan Pass connecting Sibi with Quetta.
  • Kirthar Range: forms the border between Sindh and Balochistan. Gorakh Hill near Dadu is its hill station, sometimes called the "Murree of Sindh".
  • Chagai and Ras Koh Hills: in north-western Balochistan. Pakistan's nuclear tests of 28 May 1998 were carried out in the Ras Koh Hills, Chagai district. The area also holds the Reko Diq and Saindak copper-gold deposits.
  • Makran ranges: the Central Makran and Makran Coast ranges run parallel to the coast in southern Balochistan, around Hingol National Park.

Salt Range

The Salt Range rises from the plains of Punjab between the Jhelum and Indus rivers, with the Potohar Plateau to its north.

  • Khewra Salt Mine: Pakistan's largest and oldest salt mine, often described as the world's second-largest.
  • Sakesar (about 1,522 m): the highest point of the Salt Range.
  • Also home to Kallar Kahar lake and the Katas Raj temples. Its exposed rock layers and fossils make it a famous site for geologists.

Practice MCQs: 25 questions

Tap a bubble to answer. You will see the right answer and why straight away.

Score 0 of 250 answered

  1. 1. Which is the highest peak in Pakistan?

    Answer: B) K2. At 8,611 m it is also the second-highest peak in the world.

  2. 2. K2 is located in which mountain range?

    Answer: C) Karakoram. It sits on the Pakistan-China border in Gilgit-Baltistan.

  3. 3. Nanga Parbat belongs to which range?

    Answer: B) Himalaya. It marks the western end of the Great Himalaya.

  4. 4. What is the highest peak of the Hindu Kush?

    Answer: B) Tirich Mir. At 7,708 m, it stands in Chitral. Rakaposhi is in the Karakoram.

  5. 5. How many of the world's fourteen 8,000-metre peaks are in Pakistan?

    Answer: C) 5. K2, Nanga Parbat, Gasherbrum I, Broad Peak and Gasherbrum II.

  6. 6. Which is the longest glacier in the Karakoram?

    Answer: C) Siachen. Siachen is about 76 km long. Biafo and Baltoro come next.

  7. 7. The Khunjerab Pass connects Pakistan with which country?

    Answer: B) China. The Karakoram Highway crosses the border here.

  8. 8. Which peak is known as the "Killer Mountain"?

    Answer: B) Nanga Parbat. Don't mix it up with K2, which is called the "Savage Mountain".

  9. 9. The Khyber Pass lies in which range?

    Answer: B) Safed Koh. Safed Koh is also called Spin Ghar. The pass links Peshawar with Torkham and Kabul.

  10. 10. The Bolan Pass connects which two places?

    Answer: B) Sibi and Quetta. Quetta and Chaman are linked by the Khojak Pass instead.

  11. 11. The Khojak Pass is in which range?

    Answer: B) Toba Kakar. The Khojak railway tunnel runs beneath it.

  12. 12. The hill station Fort Munro lies in which range?

    Answer: B) Sulaiman. It is near Dera Ghazi Khan.

  13. 13. The Shandur Pass connects:

    Answer: A) Chitral and Gilgit. It hosts the famous Shandur polo festival.

  14. 14. Which range forms the border between Sindh and Balochistan?

    Answer: B) Kirthar. It runs north to south along the provincial boundary.

  15. 15. Gorakh Hill, the "Murree of Sindh", is part of which range?

    Answer: A) Kirthar. It lies near Dadu.

  16. 16. Pakistan's nuclear tests of 28 May 1998 were carried out in:

    Answer: B) Ras Koh Hills, Chagai. The Ras Koh Hills are in Chagai district, north-western Balochistan.

  17. 17. The Khewra Salt Mine is located in:

    Answer: B) Salt Range. It is in Jhelum district, Punjab.

  18. 18. What is the highest point of the Salt Range?

    Answer: B) Sakesar. Sakesar is about 1,522 m. Tilla Jogian is a well-known but lower hill in the same range.

  19. 19. The Salt Range lies between which two rivers?

    Answer: C) Jhelum and Indus. The Potohar Plateau lies to its north.

  20. 20. The Margalla Hills are part of:

    Answer: B) Himalayan foothills. They rise on the northern edge of Islamabad.

  21. 21. The Lowari Pass connects:

    Answer: A) Dir and Chitral. The Lowari Tunnel now keeps the route open in winter.

  22. 22. Rakaposhi overlooks which valley?

    Answer: B) Hunza-Nagar. It is a Karakoram peak, visible from the Karakoram Highway.

  23. 23. In which year was K2 first climbed?

    Answer: B) 1954. An Italian expedition made the first ascent. 1953 is the year of Nanga Parbat's first ascent, a common trap.

  24. 24. The Reko Diq copper-gold deposit is located in:

    Answer: A) Chagai district. It is in Balochistan, close to the Saindak project.

  25. 25. The Gomal Pass lies in which region?

    Answer: B) Waziristan Hills. It links Dera Ismail Khan with Afghanistan.
